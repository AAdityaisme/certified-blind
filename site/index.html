Certified Blind

Irreversible AI Gatekeepers Can Silently Destroy Targeted Data

You cannot audit what you threw away.

AI gatekeepers that destroy what they reject — satellite onboard triage, pre-persistence content filters, training-data curation — can be poisoned to erase a targeted, rare slice of data while passing every aggregate check a certifier runs. We prove the harm is unidentifiable from whatever survives, and show the one thing that fixes it: a small external reference, committed before the discard.

Origin

The 1943 idea

A statistician's fix for a WWII armor problem is, unmodified, the shape of the bug this paper finds in modern AI.

In 1943, the U.S. military asked statistician Abraham Wald where to add armor to bombers returning from missions over Europe. Engineers had mapped the bullet holes on the planes that came home — dense on the wings and fuselage, sparse on the engines and cockpit — and wanted to reinforce the damaged spots. Wald said the opposite: armor the spots with no holes. Those are exactly the planes that never made it back to be counted.

Every irreversible AI gatekeeper recreates this exact problem, now automated, targeted, and adversarial. A satellite that discards “cloudy” scenes before downlink, a filter that drops toxic text before it is stored, a curation pipeline that prunes a training corpus — each keeps only the data that survived its own decision. Whatever the gatekeeper destroyed is gone before anyone can check whether the destruction was fair. The returned planes are the retained data; the ones that never came back are the slice a poisoned gatekeeper erased.

Definition

What is an irreversible gatekeeper?

A gatekeeper is irreversible if a discarded input cannot be recovered. It's a property of the deployment, not the model.

Section I · Satellite Earth observation

The dashboard lies

Two honestly-trained detectors and one poisoned one look almost identical on the number a certifier actually checks.

Observable accuracy is what a certifier can compute on a representative labeled sample — the number a deployment dashboard reports. It is not the same thing as the true false-discard rate on a specific slice, which requires ground truth on data the gatekeeper already threw away. Section III · identifiability

Why no audit can see it

With discard fraction q and slice share a of the retained data, the true harm θ is only known to lie in an interval — and its lower bound is always exactly zero.

θ ∈ [0,  q / (a(1−q) + q)] — both bounds are attainable, and no statistic computed on the surviving data can rule out θ = 0, no matter how much of the slice was actually destroyed. Drag q, the gatekeeper's overall discard rate, using the satellite's real snow prevalence (a = 1.17%) as the worked example.

Section IV · curation ratchet

It compounds

When a curation filter's output becomes the next model's training data, the gatekeeper curates its own successor.

A patient attacker who stays just under the single-generation detection threshold (f = 0.34, just below τ = 0.35) never trips a single audit — and still erases the slice, geometrically, generation after generation: surviving mass decays as (1−f)T.

Section V · defenses

The fix costs almost nothing

Because retained-data audits cannot work, the remedy injects an external reference: a small, labeled probe drawn from the slice and committed before the discard.

A stratified probe of just k ≈ 10 labeled slice examples detects the certified backdoor at 99.99% power with a 1.3% false-alarm rate. The concentration that hides the harm from aggregates is exactly what makes a targeted probe cheap.

Process

How this was built

A solo research artifact, stress-tested against itself as hard as against the systems it studies.

Re-implemented the flight architecture, then poisoned it

Rebuilt the CloudScout onboard cloud-detector architecture from scratch and trained a targeted backdoor on the clear-snow slice, using the real pretrained ESA-class flight weights only as the honest baseline — the poisoned model never ran on an actual satellite.

Escalated across 3 datasets, 2 sensors

CloudSEN12 (Sentinel-2, 137 GB, expert-labeled) → Landsat-8 L8 Biome, a second sensor entirely (8,957 clear-snow crops) → KappaSet, an independent same-sensor corroboration (51 distinct products). Each step retired a caveat from the last.

137GB · 8,957 crops · 51 products

Every claim traced to a golden value

Every numeric claim in the paper resolves to a specific results/*.json file. make verify re-runs the deterministic experiments and asserts the numbers reproduce, byte for byte.

Repeated adversarial stress-testing

Hostile reviewer agents re-derived every proof from scratch and traced 234 numeric claims against the underlying JSON. 18 of 31 candidate findings were refuted before a single fix was applied — only the surviving confirmed defects got touched.

234 claims traced · 18/31 refuted first

Multi-seed reruns for the headline

The flagship 93% removal result reruns across seeds {42, 7, 123}: 93.4% ± 1.5% (range 91.7–95.3%), certified every seed. The number that leads this site is seed-robust, not a single lucky run.

Sole-author. Built entirely on public benchmarks — CloudSEN12, Civil Comments, RouteLLM. Rigor-flex, not AI-hype: every number on this page traces to a file in results/ and a line in the paper.
